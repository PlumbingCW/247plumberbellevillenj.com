Plumbing-side boiler problems we handle

A boiler is part of the plumbing system. Water leaks, failed valves, pressure problems and waterlogged expansion tanks are all plumbing issues.

If the problem is the burner, the controls or combustion, we say so and recommend the right contractor instead of guessing.

A boiler with a leaking body is usually at end of life.

Expansion tanks

An expansion tank gives heated water room to expand. When the bladder fails, the tank fills with water and pressure spikes, which opens the relief valve and wets the floor.

Replacing the tank is a common repair and often fixes repeated pressure problems. We check the air charge and the connection before and after.

Winter safety

Never ignore the smell of gas. Leave the house and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734, then call 911 if the smell is strong.

Install a carbon monoxide alarm near the boiler and on each floor. Boilers burning gas or oil produce carbon monoxide if the venting or combustion is faulty. Have the boiler serviced each year.

Before you call

Quick checks save time.

  • Check that the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh.
  • Look at the pressure gauge, which should usually read between 12 and 15 psi when cold.
  • Check the power switch and the breaker.
  • Look for water on the floor and note where it comes from.
  • Write down any error code or blinking light.

Steam or hot water: know which you have

A hot water boiler pushes heated water through pipes to radiators or baseboard. A steam boiler boils water and sends steam up to radiators, where it condenses and runs back down. Steam systems have a sight glass on the boiler and radiator valves that hiss.

The difference matters because the faults differ. Steam systems fail on low water cutoffs, clogged returns and stuck vents. Hot water systems fail on circulators, zone valves and pressure. Tell us which you have when you call.

Keeping a boiler healthy through winter

Check the pressure gauge monthly in the heating season. Keep storage away from the boiler, leave space for service and never stack boxes against it. Bleed radiators in the fall if they stay cold at the top.

A yearly service by a heating contractor catches burner and combustion problems early. For the plumbing side, a quick look at valves, the expansion tank and visible fittings can head off a leak.

Standards for boiler plumbing

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. It covers relief valve discharge piping, backflow protection on the fill line and the expansion tank connection. Burner and venting rules sit in separate fuel gas and mechanical codes, which is why we hand those to a heating contractor.
