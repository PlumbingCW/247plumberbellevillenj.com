Toilet overflowing emergency: stop it now

If the bowl is rising, act before it spills.

  • Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until water stops.
  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down if the tank is still filling.
  • Do not flush again, and do not plunge a bowl that is already at the rim.
  • Lay towels around the base and move rugs.
  • Switch off power to outlets near standing water.

Then call (973) 488-5410. We dispatch 24/7, and if the overflow came from the main line we say so on the phone.

A clog in one toilet is a branch issue. If several fixtures back up, see sewer line cleaning.

Clogged toilets

A plunger solves most clogs. Use a flange plunger and push slowly. If the bowl does not clear after a few tries, a closet auger reaches the trap without scratching the porcelain.

When repeated clogs happen, the cause may be a partial blockage in the branch line or an old low-flow model that flushes poorly. We check before replacing anything.

Leaks at the base

Water around the base usually comes from a failed wax ring. The ring seals the toilet to the drain flange, and when it fails, wastewater seeps into the subfloor.

Do not ignore it. A slow leak rots flooring and can drip through the ceiling below. We pull the toilet, check the flange, replace the seal and reset the toilet level and secure.

Running toilets and the water bill

A toilet that runs can waste a large amount of water, and it does not announce itself. A simple test is to add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks.

Belleville bills water through the Belleville Water Department at 973-450-3414. If your bill rises, a running toilet is one of the first things to check.

Code points for toilets

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. It sets clearances around a toilet, flange and vent requirements and the flush volume limits for new fixtures. A like-for-like repair rarely triggers changes, but a new toilet or a moved drain must meet them.

Insurance note: a sudden overflow that damages floors may be covered, while a slow leak from a worn seal often is not. Photograph everything before cleanup.
