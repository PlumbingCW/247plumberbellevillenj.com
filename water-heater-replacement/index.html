Water heater emergency: when it cannot wait

A tank that leaks from the seam is finished. Water from the fittings or the relief valve can sometimes be repaired, but water pooling under the body means the steel has rusted through.

Act fast if the tank is in a finished basement or above a ceiling, because a few gallons spread quickly.

  • Turn off the cold water supply at the valve above the tank.
  • Switch the gas valve to pilot or off, or flip the breaker for an electric unit.
  • Open a hot tap upstairs to let pressure out, and run a hose from the drain valve to a floor drain if you can do it safely.
  • Never touch wet electrical parts or standing water near a powered tank.

Then call (973) 488-5410. We dispatch around the clock, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Repair or replace: how we decide

A heater that is a few years old with a bad thermostat or element is worth fixing. Our water heater repair page covers those failures in detail.

Replace when the tank leaks, when repair parts would cost more than a third of a new unit, or when the heater has struggled for years and the sediment has hardened. A newer unit also recovers faster and loses less heat standing idle.

We give you both numbers when the answer is close, so the decision is yours.

Belleville water and your tank

Belleville buys treated water from the Newark Water Department, which draws from the Pequannock watershed. The 2023 report listed hardness at 48.7 ppm and pH 7.55, which is gentle on a steel tank compared with hard-water towns.

Soft, mildly alkaline water mostly means the anode rod does its job for longer and heating elements stay cleaner. It does not stop a tank from aging, and we still see failures from simple wear.

If your home still has an old lead service line, a new water heater does not change that. Read our water service line page if your home was built before 1950.

How a same-day replacement runs

A typical swap takes a few hours when the old connections are sound.

  1. Shut down and drainWe close the water, cut gas or power, and drain the old tank to a floor drain or outdoors.
  2. Remove and prepareThe old tank comes out. We inspect the shutoff valves, gas connector, venting and drain pan before anything new goes in.
  3. Set and connectThe new unit gets new supply lines, a temperature and pressure relief valve with proper discharge piping, and an expansion tank when the system is closed.
  4. Test and walk throughWe fire the unit, check for leaks and combustion safety, set the temperature, and show you the shutoffs.

If we find a failed shutoff valve or a corroded gas connector, we tell you the price before replacing it.

What we check before we quote

A good quote starts with a short checklist. Have these ready when you call.

  • Fuel type and tank size printed on the old label.
  • Where the tank sits and how tight the space is.
  • Whether the unit vents through a chimney or direct vent.
  • Age of the unit and any recent leaks or rumbling.
  • Whether your home has an expansion tank on the incoming line.

Photos of the label and the surrounding pipes help us bring the right unit on the first visit.

What drives the cost of replacing a water heater

You get a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

  • Tank type and sizeA 40 gallon standard tank costs less than a 50 gallon unit, and both cost less than tankless.
  • Fuel and ventingSwitching fuel or rerouting a flue adds labor and materials.
  • Code upgradesA failed relief valve discharge, a missing pan or an old gas connector may need replacing.
  • AccessA tight crawlspace or a stair climb lengthens the job.
  • Haul-away and permitDisposal and any local permit are part of the job. Ask the Belleville township office at 973-450-3300 which permits your work needs.

Insurance note: sudden tank bursts are often covered for resulting water damage but not for the tank itself. Wear and rust are not covered, so photograph the damage before cleanup.
