Replacement versus first-time install

If a disposal is already under the sink, the plumber removes the old unit, transfers the drain connections and mounts the new one. The mounting ring may be reusable, and the wiring is already in place.

A first-time install is different. The strainer flange in the sink must be replaced, the drain layout may need changes, a switch and outlet may be needed, and the dishwasher connection has to be planned. Each step adds time.

Horsepower and noise

A larger motor grinds tougher waste and jams less. It also costs more and may be heavier, which matters in a thin sink. Sound insulation is the other feature that raises the price. A quiet model is worth it in an open kitchen.

Pick for how you cook. A household that prepares large meals needs more than one that mostly rinses plates.

What a complete quote includes

Look for these items.

  • The disposal unit, with model and horsepower named.
  • A new mounting assembly if the old one is worn.
  • A new drain gasket and discharge elbow.
  • Connection to the dishwasher drain, with the knockout removed.
  • Electrical hookup, plug or hardwired as local code allows.
  • A test run and a leak check before the plumber leaves.

Ask what happens to the old unit. Most plumbers haul it away.

Electrical work

Many disposals plug into an outlet under the sink, controlled by a wall switch. Some are hardwired. If the outlet and switch are missing, an electrician or a plumber with the proper license must add them.

Ask who handles the electrical part. In New Jersey, plumbers and electricians are licensed separately, so a plumber may bring in an electrician for new circuits.

Drain condition matters

A new disposal does not fix a clogged drain. If the kitchen branch is slow, grease is the likely cause. Installing a new unit onto a clogged line only moves the problem.

We check the drain before installing, and clear it if needed. See drain cleaning for what that involves.

Choosing the right disposal for your household

A small household that cooks simply can get by with a basic model. A family that cooks daily benefits from a stronger motor and better sound insulation. Look at the grinding stages, the material of the grind chamber and the length of the warranty.

Stainless grind components resist corrosion better than coated parts. A unit with a lifetime-style warranty on the motor is worth reading closely, since terms differ.

Septic systems and disposals

If your home is on septic, ask a plumber or the septic service before adding a disposal. It adds solids to the tank and can shorten the pumping interval. Most Belleville homes are on the public sewer, but confirm yours if you are unsure.

After the install: first-week care

Run cold water for thirty seconds before and after each use. Avoid fibrous waste for the first week while you learn how the unit handles loads. Check the cabinet floor under the unit after a few days for any slow drip at the connections.

If you notice a leak or a hum that does not stop, switch the unit off and call. Early fixes are usually a quick tightening of a gasket.

Questions to ask the plumber before you hire

Ask whether the quote includes a new mounting assembly and discharge parts, or reuses the old ones. Ask who handles the dishwasher knockout plug and whether they test the dishwasher drain afterward. Ask what the labor warranty covers and for how long.

Ask how they will protect the cabinet floor and what happens to the old unit. A plumber who answers each of these plainly is likely to do tidy work.

When a disposal is not the answer

A disposal is not always the right choice. If your kitchen drain already clogs often, or the branch line is old cast iron, adding a disposal can make things worse. Composting food waste or scraping plates into the trash costs nothing and keeps grease out of the pipes.

We tell you honestly if your drain suits a disposal. If it does not, we fix the drain first.

Electrical and plumbing rules

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Disposal wiring is regulated by the National Electrical Code (NEC) and may need a licensed electrician if an outlet or circuit is missing.

Belleville note: rules and shared stacks

Ask the Belleville township office at 973-450-3300 whether a permit or inspection applies to a disposal install. In two-family houses with a shared kitchen stack, grease from one unit can clog the drain for both, so keep fats out of the sink.

Belleville's water is soft by the 2023 report, listed at 48.7 ppm hardness, so scale in the disposal is less of an issue here. Food buildup and grease cause most problems.
