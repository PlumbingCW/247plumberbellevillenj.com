Commercial plumbing emergency: protect your business first

A plumbing failure in a business can shut you down. These steps limit the loss.

  • Close the main water shutoff if water is flowing where it should not.
  • Stop kitchen and restroom use if the sewer is backing up.
  • Move stock, paper and electronics away from wet areas.
  • Photograph damage and note the time it started.
  • Tell the landlord or property manager if the building is leased.

Grease traps and interceptors

A grease trap catches fats before they reach the sewer. When it fills, it stops working, and grease goes down the line. A backed-up kitchen on a Friday night is a common result.

We service traps and interceptors, check the baffles and inlet and outlet lines, and tell you how often yours needs attention. Pumping and disposal of the contents is handled by a licensed hauler, and we help coordinate it.

Check with the township at 973-450-3300 about local grease trap and food-service requirements for your business.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer stops used water from flowing back into the clean supply. Many commercial properties, fire lines and irrigation systems use one, and most require periodic testing.

Rules on who may test and how often vary by device and by water supplier. Ask Belleville Water at 973-450-3414 what applies to your property, and we will schedule the work around it.

Codes for businesses

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Commercial work adds backflow, grease interceptor and fixture-count requirements that depend on the building and its use. Ask the township at 973-450-3300 which apply to yours, and note that any electrical work on equipment falls under the National Electrical Code (NEC) and a licensed electrician.
