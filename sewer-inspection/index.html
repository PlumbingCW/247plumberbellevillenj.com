Sewer Camera Inspection in Belleville, NJ

.

A sewer camera inspection in Belleville, NJ sends a waterproof camera through your drain line from a cleanout to the street connection and records what it sees. The video shows cracks, roots, sags, offsets, grease and collapsed sections, so you decide on repairs with real evidence instead of a guess.

Our line is open 24/7. Most inspections are planned, such as before a purchase, but we also run cameras the same day when a backup is already happening.

When a camera inspection is worth it

These are the common reasons people call.

  • You are buying a house and want to know the condition of the line before closing.
  • A drain clogs repeatedly and cabling only helps for a short time.
  • You have an older home with clay, cast iron or fiber pipe.
  • Large trees stand near the line.
  • You want proof of the condition before a repair or lining job.
  • A previous repair needs to be checked.

Our hydro jetting service often starts with exactly this camera pass.

What the camera sees

The camera head has lights and a sealed lens on a flexible cable. The operator advances it slowly, watches the live feed, and records the video. A locator on the camera head lets the crew find its position above ground, which gives you depth and distance for any defect.

We mark the location of each problem on a simple sketch, so a later repair crew can find it again.

What we look for and why it matters

Each finding points to a different next step.

Pre-purchase inspections

A home inspection rarely covers the buried sewer line. A camera does. If you are buying an older house, ask whether a cleanout exists, and schedule the camera before your contract deadline so you have time to negotiate.

You get the video and a plain-language summary of what we saw. We do not tell you to walk away from a house. We tell you what the pipe looks like and what a repair would involve.

How the visit works

A typical inspection takes about an hour.

  1. Locate the access pointWe find the cleanout or pull a toilet if no cleanout exists.
  2. Run the cameraWe advance the camera to the main and record the whole run.
  3. Review with youWe show you the footage, point out each defect and mark depths.
  4. Written summaryYou receive the video and a list of findings, with repair options if any are needed.

Standards for the findings

A camera report is only useful if it is read against a standard. Pipe slope, joint alignment and cleanout access are set out in the plumbing subcode, New Jersey's subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. We compare what the camera shows with those requirements when we explain a defect.

What changes the price of an inspection

Inspections are priced by the visit, and we tell you the price before we start.

  • AccessA working cleanout is faster than pulling a toilet.
  • Line lengthA long run to the street takes more time.
  • Cleaning needed firstA line packed with grease or roots may need cleaning before the camera can pass.
  • Locating and mappingSurface marking adds time but helps any later repair.
  • TimingSame-day or after-hours visits cost more than a planned weekday one.

Insurance note: inspections are usually not covered by homeowners policies. If a claim is possible, ask your insurer whether they want to see the video.
