Kearny's low ground and what it means for plumbing

Kearny sits between the Passaic and Hackensack rivers on low ground. Groundwater runs high, storm drains work hard, and basements flood more easily than in hillier towns.

That makes a working sump pump and clear storm drains the first line of defense, and it makes a backup power source for the pump worth having.

Flood readiness for Kearny homeowners

On low ground, the best protection is a layered plan. A primary pump, a battery backup, a clear discharge line and a working check valve each remove a point of failure. A floor drain with a backwater valve adds another.

Move valuables and electronics off the basement floor, and keep the electrical panel and water heater on a raised platform where possible.

After the water recedes

Once a flood has passed, dry the basement quickly. Run fans and a dehumidifier, discard soaked drywall and insulation, and clean hard surfaces. Mold can start within a couple of days.

Have the plumbing checked afterward. Floods can crack drains, loosen fittings and fill the sump with silt. We inspect the pump, the discharge and the visible pipes.

Industrial neighbors and mixed streets

Kearny blends houses, apartments and small industrial and commercial buildings. A shop on a street with homes may share a drain with them. We ask about the building type and the shared lines when you call.

Code we work to in Kearny

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. That includes backwater protection and discharge rules that matter on low ground, where a sump discharge must go outdoors and never to the sanitary sewer.
