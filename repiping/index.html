Repiping in Belleville, NJ

.

Repiping in Belleville, NJ is the replacement of a home's old supply pipes with new copper or PEX, done when galvanized steel or aging copper starts to leak, rust or lose pressure. Repiping is planned work that fixes a problem at its root instead of patching one leak after another.

Signs your pipes may need replacing

One leak is not a repipe. A pattern is.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different places.
  • Rust-colored or metallic-tasting water.
  • Low pressure that does not improve after fixture repairs.
  • Visible corrosion or green scale on copper.
  • Galvanized steel pipe in a house built before the 1960s.
  • Flaking and scaling inside pipes when a section is cut open.

Take photos of each leak and note the date. It shows the pattern.

Pipe materials compared

Each has strengths and limits.

CopperLong track record, durable, fire resistantHigher material cost, can corrode in aggressive water.

Full repipe or partial

A full repipe replaces every supply line in the house. A partial repipe replaces the worst sections, such as a basement run or a single bathroom.

A partial fix costs less today but may leave weak pipes elsewhere. We show you which pipes look worst and what the options are.

Water quality in Belleville

Belleville's 2023 water quality report listed hardness at 48.7 ppm, which is soft. Soft water can be mildly aggressive toward some metals, while treatment helps protect pipes. Ask the Belleville Water Department at 973-450-3414 for the current report.

Rusty water at the tap often comes from galvanized pipe inside the house, not from the street. A repipe removes that source.

Permits and code

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. A repipe must follow that code, and the work must be performed by a licensed master plumber or their employees.

Ask the Belleville township office at 973-450-3300 which permits and inspections apply to a repipe at your address.

How a repipe is done

We plan around your life so you are not without water for long.

  1. Inspect and planWe trace the lines and agree on a route and the access points.
  2. Rough in new pipeWe run new lines while the old ones still work.
  3. Tie inWe switch over fixture by fixture, then remove the old lines.
  4. Test and inspectWe pressure test and arrange any required inspection.
  5. Patch accessWe leave access openings ready for drywall repair.

Living in the house during a repipe

Most families stay home during a repipe. Plan for short water shutoffs while sections are tied in, a few days of crew activity and some dust from access openings. Cover furniture, clear closets and tell the crew about pets.

Ask the contractor for a day-by-day plan. Knowing which bathroom is out of service on which day makes the week easy to manage.

Protecting your investment after the job

New pipe lasts longer when pressure is controlled. A pressure reducing valve keeps pressure in a safe range, and an expansion tank protects against thermal spikes. Know where the main shutoff is and test it twice a year.

Keep the permit paperwork and the invoice with your house records. They help at sale and with insurance.

What to do if a pipe fails before the repipe: safety steps

Close the main shutoff, open the lowest faucet to relieve pressure and switch off power to any wet area. Do not touch outlets or fixtures with wet hands. Take photos, then call.

Until the job is done, check the pipes weekly for new stains or drips, and keep the main shutoff clear and easy to reach.
