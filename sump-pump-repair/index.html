Sump pump emergency: what to do while water rises

Act on safety first, then on the water.

  • Do not step into standing water if the basement has live outlets or the panel is low.
  • Unplug the pump only if you can reach the outlet from dry ground.
  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Lift the float by hand to see if the pump runs.
  • Move belongings off the floor and take photos for your insurer.

Common sump pump failures

Most failures come down to a few causes.

FailureCauseFix.
Pump will not turn onFloat stuck, switch failed or no powerFree or replace the float or switch.
Runs but pumps littleClogged intake or failed impellerClean the intake or replace the pump.
Runs constantlyStuck float or failed check valveReset the float, replace the valve.
Noisy or vibratingWorn bearings or debrisClean or replace.
Water returns after it stopsMissing or failed check valveInstall a check valve.

A pump that is ten years old or more should be replaced before it fails.

Battery backup and water-powered backups

Storms cut power, and a pump without power does nothing. A battery backup pump runs from a deep-cycle battery when the main pump loses power. A water-powered backup uses municipal water pressure and needs no battery, though it uses water.

We size the backup to the pit and the typical inflow, and test it. Batteries need to be tested and replaced on schedule. For more on options see basement waterproofing.

Why Belleville basements flood

Belleville sits along the Second River and near the Passaic River floodplain, and heavy rain raises ground water around foundations. Older homes often have no pit at all, or a pit with a small pump that was never replaced.

Ask the Belleville township office at 973-450-3300 about local flood maps and any requirements for discharge. Never connect a sump discharge to the sanitary sewer. Discharge outdoors, away from the foundation.

Test your pump before the storm

A five minute check is worth it.

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and shuts off.
  • Check the discharge line is clear and drains away from the house.
  • Look for a check valve on the discharge line.
  • Confirm the pump plugs into a working outlet, ideally a GFCI.
  • Test the battery backup if you have one.

If any of these fail, call us before the next storm.

How a replacement works

A pump swap is a quick job once the pit is clear.

  1. Inspect the pitWe check the pit, the discharge line and the electrical supply.
  2. Remove the old pumpWe disconnect it, clean the pit and remove debris.
  3. Install the new pumpWe set it level, add a check valve and connect the discharge.
  4. TestWe fill the pit and watch it cycle.

What to do while a pump is down: safety steps

Never wade into a flooded basement if the water could reach outlets, the panel or an appliance, because electric shock is the main danger. Wear boots if you must enter, stay out if the water is above the outlets and call the electric utility if the panel is wet.

Pump wiring and outlets fall under the National Electrical Code (NEC), which calls for ground-fault protection on basement receptacles. New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. The discharge must go outdoors and never to the sanitary sewer.
