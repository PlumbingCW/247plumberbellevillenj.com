What makes plumbing in Newark different

Newark has one of the oldest and densest housing stocks in New Jersey. Many buildings have cast iron stacks, clay laterals and shared walls, and a failure in one often reaches the next.

Large buildings and multi-family houses mean a clog is often a shared-line problem, and a repair needs coordination between units.

Aging pipes in older Newark buildings

Cast iron stacks and galvanized supply lines that date back many decades are common. They corrode from the inside, so the first sign is often rust-colored water or a leak at a fitting.

A camera shows pipe condition, and for repeated failures a larger fix may cost less over time. See sewer camera inspection and repiping.

Large buildings and shared systems

Newark has many three-family houses and small apartment buildings with one boiler, one water heater and one building drain. A failure there affects every household at once, so speed and clear communication matter.

We talk to the owner, the manager or the tenant who called, whichever is available, and explain what is needed before starting. Written notes and photos go to the owner afterward.

What to tell us when you call

Give the address and the nearest cross street, which floor is affected, and whether water or sewage is involved. Say if the building has a cleanout in the basement. Tell us if access needs a key or a code.

That information lets us send the right equipment and gets us into the building without delay.

Code we work to in Newark

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. For older buildings with mixed pipe materials we match new work to the code instead of the old pipe, and we say where a full upgrade would be wiser than a patch.
