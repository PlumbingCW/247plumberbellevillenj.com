Burst pipe emergency: do these first

Speed matters more than anything else.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement.
  • Open the lowest faucet and the highest one to drain the system.
  • Switch off power to wet areas at the panel if you can reach it without standing in water.
  • Turn off the water heater to protect it from running dry.
  • Move valuables and take photos before you start cleanup.

Then call (973) 488-5410. We dispatch 24/7, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Frozen pipes

Pipes freeze where they run through cold spaces: exterior walls, unheated basements, crawlspaces, attics and garages. A frozen pipe may not burst until it thaws, and the flood begins when the ice melts.

If a faucet trickles or gives nothing in freezing weather, a pipe may be frozen. Keep the faucet open, warm the area with a space heater placed safely, and do not use an open flame. If you see a crack or a bulge, close the main and call.

Why pipes burst

The cause affects the fix and how likely it is to happen again.

CauseHow it shows upFix.
FreezingCold snap, no flow, a split along the pipeReplace the section and insulate.
Corrosion in old galvanized or copperPinhole leaks that become a splitReplace the section, consider repiping.
High water pressureBanging pipes and repeated leaksInstall or adjust a pressure reducing valve.
Physical damageNail, drill or impactRepair the section.
Failed joint or fittingLeak at a connectionRemake the joint.

Repeated leaks are a signal. See repiping when pipes are failing in several places.

After the water stops

Standing water and wet materials need attention within a day or two. Drywall, insulation and flooring can grow mold quickly when they stay damp.

We repair the pipe and tell you what we see, but drying out and restoring the structure is a separate trade. We can recommend what to ask your insurer, and we document the damage with photos and notes for your claim.

Protecting pipes before winter

Insulate pipes in exposed spaces. Seal drafts around pipes where they pass through the foundation. Disconnect garden hoses and close and drain outdoor faucets before the first hard freeze.

In a cold snap, let a faucet drip, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, and keep the heat set above freezing even when you are away. Know where your main shutoff is before you need it.

Repairs must meet code

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. A burst section replaced with new pipe must be supported, sized and joined as that code requires. We pressure test every repair before we close it up.
