Main line backup emergency: first steps

A blocked main makes the lowest drain in the house the overflow point.

  • Stop all water use: no showers, laundry, dishwashers or flushing.
  • Keep family and pets away from any wastewater on the floor.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaners into a full line.
  • Take photos of the backup and any damaged items for your insurer.
  • Locate your cleanout if you can and keep the area clear for the crew.

Then call (973) 488-5410. We dispatch 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Main line, branch line or fixture clog

A single sink or toilet that will not drain is a branch problem. When two or more fixtures back up together, or when the basement floor drain bubbles, the main line is the suspect.

Telling the two apart saves money. We ask which fixtures are affected and how the drains behave when water runs elsewhere, and often can say whether it is the main before we arrive.

Storm drains and yard drains

Roof leaders, area drains and driveway drains fill with silt and leaves, then fail in the first heavy rain. Basement flooding that follows a storm sometimes comes from a blocked yard drain rather than from the sewer.

We clear these lines, check where they discharge, and tell you if they connect to the house drain in a way that should be corrected. Belleville's streets near the Second River see fast runoff, so clean outdoor drains matter here.

Keeping the line clear afterward

Grease belongs in the trash, not the sink. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper, and they are a leading cause of main line clogs.

Enzyme and bacteria products can help with light organic buildup, but they do not cut roots or clear a stopped line. A yearly camera or jet cleaning is a better plan for older lines or properties with big trees.

A cleanout that is accessible and capped saves time in an emergency. If yours is buried or missing, we can add one during the visit.

Why cleanout access matters

New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, through N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15. Under that code a building drain needs accessible cleanouts. If yours is buried or missing, we can add one during the visit so the next cleaning is faster and cheaper.
